## Engineering a Selective and Thermostable Borneol Dehydrogenase

**SrBDH1** is an NAD+-dependent oxidoreductase from *Salvia rosmarinus* that catalyzes the oxidation of borneol into camphor, a valuable monoterpenoid used in fragrances, pharmaceuticals, and fine chemicals.

This project combines **ancestral sequence reconstruction**, **AI-driven protein design**, and **multiscale molecular simulations** to overcome the classical stability–function tradeoff in enzyme engineering.

Our goal is to improve thermal robustness while retaining the native preference for **(+)-borneol**, and to understand how enzyme dynamics regulate enantioselectivity.

### Highlights

- **Designed variants with enhanced thermostability** while preserving catalytic function.
- **Retained native stereoselectivity** toward (+)-borneol.
- **Hybrid ML/MM simulations** revealed productive catalytic binding modes.
- **Tyr159 identified** as a major contributor to selective substrate recognition.
- Demonstrated that **protein dynamics govern selectivity** beyond static structures.

### Methods

- Ancestral Sequence Reconstruction (ASR)
- Full-atom message passing neural networks (FAMPNN)
- Molecular dynamics simulations
- Hybrid ML/MM simulations
- Binding free energy decomposition

### Scientific Impact

This work establishes an integrated framework for rational enzyme optimization using machine learning, evolution, and atomistic simulations, with applications in sustainable biocatalysis.
